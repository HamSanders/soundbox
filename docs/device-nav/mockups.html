Three ways to get around Mirli

Each model below covers a phone (375 px), an iPad in portrait (768 px) and a desktop window (1280 px). The frames are drawn at their real pixel sizes and scaled to fit. They are clickable: tap the nav, a sound row's ⋯, the Create button, and open a studio project. The visual style is Mirli's current one; only the navigation and menus change.

Devices

Common to all three

  • Every tap target is at least 44 px on touch screens, chosen by (pointer: coarse), not by screen width. A touch laptop or an iPad with a trackpad gets the right size either way.
  • Context menus become bottom sheets on touch, opened by the visible ⋯ button or by long-press. Every item stays reachable without the gesture.
  • Hover-only controls (row actions that fade in) always show on touch.
  • Items that can't work in the browser studio stay visible but say why ("Desktop app only"), as Apple asks for tabs: explain the state, don't hide or grey it without a reason.
  • The mini player sits flush on top of the bottom bar, with no gap that lets taps fall through.

Breakpoints

Material's width classes: under 600 px is a phone layout, 600 to 839 is medium (iPad portrait), 840 and up is expanded (iPad landscape, laptop). Mirli today switches only at Tailwind's md (768 px), so an iPad mini in portrait (744 px) gets the phone layout and a 768 px iPad gets the desktop one.
